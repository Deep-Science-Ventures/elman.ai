How it works

Decomposition, then evidence — not a black-box verdict.

Conventional FTO tooling scores a whole filing for similarity against a patent corpus and returns a number. That number is not defensible: it can't tell you which component of your candidate reads on which limitation of which claim, and it can't show you the sentence that justifies the call.

Elman inverts this. It decomposes your candidate into its constituent components, then assesses each one against the relevant claims of each patent independently. Every verdict resolves to a specific claim limitation and the supporting passage — the evidence an attorney needs to agree or push back.

Per-component decomposition

A claim-level evidence trail on every verdict, mapped limitation by limitation — not a single score on the entire filing.

Defensible under review

One isolated agent per patent, with no cross-contamination between filings — so each assessment stands on its own when an IP committee interrogates it.

Auditable end to end

Every conclusion traces back to a source passage you can open, read, and challenge. The reasoning is inspectable, not asserted.

Who it's for

Built for the people who sign off on the risk.

FTO sits with patent counsel, IP committees, and R&D leadership making go/no-go calls on programmes worth hundreds of millions. Those decisions can't rest on an opaque model output. Elman gives those teams a per-claim, per-component assessment they can audit, cite, and defend — the same trail of reasoning they would build by hand, produced at machine scale.
